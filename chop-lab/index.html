Welcome to Chop Lab

  1. Hear the starter beat. Press Space or Play. Click squares in the grid to add or remove hits.
  2. Play the pads with your keyboard: 1234 · QWER · ASDF · ZXCV. Turn on Record hits to finger-drum straight into the pattern.
  3. Make it a song. Set how many bars a pattern lasts (1 to 128), and add patterns up to H. On the Song timeline under the grid, click a lane to place its pattern; stack lanes at the same bar to layer them, then switch to Play song. Each pattern plays one kit: the tabs above the pads choose it, so a verse and a chorus can sound different.
  4. Pick a genre. The Genres tab holds 63 kits across 17 families, from boom bap, trap, and house to jazz, reggae, country, and metal. Press Hear it, then Load kit to put its sounds and a starter beat on the pads.
  5. Bring your own sounds. Drop MP3, WAV, M4A, OGG, or FLAC files anywhere, or record with the mic. Drag any sound onto a pad or a deck.
  6. Chop. Open a sound in the Chop tab, chop it evenly, on hits, or by beats (or tap Enter while it plays), then press Send chops to pads.
  7. Screw. Slide Screw down to slow and deepen everything together. For a whole track, pick it in Sounds and press Screw for a chopped & screwed version.
  8. DJ. In Decks, scratch on the platter, sync the tempo, set hot cues, loop, and use Jump back and Double up for screw-style repeats.
  9. Record and share. Rec captures everything you hear into Your sounds. Chop it again, or press WAV to save a file.
  10. Make a music pack. Export music pack, under the song, saves a pattern or the song as a seamless MP3 loop in one .zip file that games and music players can stream. Save as genre loop keeps a song as a genre’s own loop, and Export all genres on the Genres tab packs every genre at once.
